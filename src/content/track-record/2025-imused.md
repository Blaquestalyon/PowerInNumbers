The pattern here is not that iMused preceded AI music generation — Suno and Udio were operating before iMused. The pattern is that iMused preceded the industry's recognition that AI music generation required a compliance architecture to be sustainable. That recognition arrived via litigation. The architecture arrived with iMused.

The RIAA filed suit against Suno and Udio in June 2024, alleging that both platforms reproduced copyrighted sound recordings without authorisation. iMused was in development during that period. By the time Suno settled with major labels in July 2025, iMused had already completed beta and was days from its public launch. The settlement required Suno to build the licensing and attribution infrastructure it had not originally included. iMused launched with that infrastructure as its founding architecture — not as a remediation.

The compliance posture is specific. Every commercial release on iMused requires Performing Rights Organisation registration — legal name, IPI number, PRO affiliation — before generation. Source artists receive a guaranteed, auditable royalty split of 10% (user-generated DNA profiles) or 20% (Official DNA profiles) on all commercially exploited works. All generations are recorded in a public, verifiable attribution ledger. The platform is designed so that a work produced on iMused and then reproduced on any other platform or studio carries its compliance status with it.

The press release, dated August 28, 2025, states the claim directly: iMused.ai launches as the first ethical AI music platform. The claim is structural. No comparable compliance-first architecture existed at public launch — the industry's closest equivalents were consent decrees and settlement terms, not founding design.
